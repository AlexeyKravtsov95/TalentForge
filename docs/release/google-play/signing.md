## 1. The scheme in one table

| Key | Who holds it | Type | Signs | Goes to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **App signing key** | the owner (and, after §5, a copy at Google Play App Signing) | RSA 4096, PKCS12, ~100-year validity | the universal APK (`assemblePublishableRelease`) | RuStore and AppGallery receive the **APK** signed with it. Google Play receives an **encrypted copy of the key** once (§5) and signs the APKs it delivers with it |
| **Upload key** | the owner only | RSA 4096 recommended (Google's minimum: RSA 2048) | the app bundle only (`bundlePublishablePlay`) | its **certificate** (public) is registered in Play Console; the bundle signed with it is uploaded |

What users install:

| Store | File the store delivers | Certificate on the user's device |
|---|---|---|
| RuStore | the owner's APK, byte-identical | app signing key |
| AppGallery | the owner's APK, byte-identical | app signing key |
| Google Play | APKs generated by Play from the bundle | app signing key (Play re-signs with the copy from §5) |

One certificate on every install means an install from any store can be updated by the same app
from any other store (Android requires the same package name and signing certificate). That holds
only if Play uses the owner's key (§3, §5). The upload key never reaches a user's device.

## 2. Why two keys

- **Google's guidance.** "For maximum security, your upload key and app signing key should be
  different" (Play App Signing help). Google allows one key for both, but recommends against it.
- **Damage control.** A leaked or lost upload key can be replaced by Play support (§7) without
  touching any installed app. A lost app signing key ends in-place updates in RuStore and
  AppGallery ([multi-store §10](../appgallery/multi-store-signing.md#10-consequences-of-losing-or-leaking-the-signing-key)).
  So the app signing key is used as rarely as possible: for the store APK and once for the
  Play hand-over.
- **No confusion.** The build and both validators make sure that each artifact carries the right
  key (§6).

## 3. Google Play's default for a new app, and the owner's choice

Google's [Play App Signing help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9842756)
(read 2026-10-19) says:

- New apps are **automatically enrolled in hybrid signing with Google-generated keys** when the
  first bundle is uploaded. Hybrid means a classical RSA 4096 key plus a post-quantum ML-DSA-65 key.
  Google also generates a separate classical key for the hybrid signature, so a hybrid app has
  **three keys**. Android 17 (API 37) and later verify with APK Signature Scheme v3.2; older devices
  use the classical signature (API 33–36 enforce the latest classical key through v3.1).
- Developers can instead **provide their own app signing key** ("for advanced developers"):
  Play Console → *Protected with Play* → *Play Store distribution* → *Go to Play app signing* →
  *Change the app signing key*. The two choices offered are reusing a key from another app in the
  account or providing a copy of your own key. Android's
  [app-signing guide](https://developer.android.com/studio/publish/app-signing) recommends providing
  your own key when the same key must be used across several stores.
- The key can be changed **only "before there is a release rolled out in open testing track or
  production track"**. After that, only the key-upgrade process applies.

**Why the default is unacceptable for TalentForge.** A Google-generated key gives Play installs a
certificate that RuStore and AppGallery installs do not have. Those users could then never move
between stores without uninstalling, and an uninstall **deletes the saved builds**
([multi-store §9](../appgallery/multi-store-signing.md#9-cross-store-installation-and-update-scenarios)).
RuStore also warns that a Google-generated Play key may not be usable outside Google Play.

**Decision required from the owner (P-1) 🔑:** provide the owner's app signing key to Play (§5).

**When.** Google names the open-testing or production rollout as the point after which the key can
no longer be changed. Internal and closed testing are not named as locking it. This document
still treats **the first bundle upload** as the deadline:

- the first upload is the moment Play enrols the app in Google-generated hybrid signing;
- Google's page does not say how a later change interacts with testers who already installed a
  Google-signed build;
- a closed-test install signed with a Google key could not be updated in place by the
  owner-signed build.

So the owner sets the key **after creating the app and before uploading the first bundle** (§5.2).

**UNVERIFIED on Google's pages (2026-10-19):**

- U-1: whether providing your own key keeps the app on classical signing or still adds
  Google-generated hybrid keys. The Google security blog says developers will "later" be able to
  bring their own classical and ML-DSA keys.
- U-2: whether hybrid enrolment can be refused at all for a new app.
- U-3: the exact current wording of the PEPK upload steps for a **new** app (the help page
  describes PEPK only for migrating an existing app).

The owner checks U-1…U-3 on the Play Console screen itself before confirming (§5.2, step 4).

**Key upgrade (not now).** Google describes an optional key upgrade (annual for Android 17+
installs) that adds a new key and a rotation lineage. Any key upgrade gives Play installs a
certificate lineage that RuStore and AppGallery installs do not have. **Do not request or accept
a key upgrade** until its effect on cross-store updates is analysed (residual risk R-3 in
[readiness](readiness.md#7-residual-risks)).

## 4. Creating the keys 🔑

Both keys are created by the owner on the owner's machine, **outside every git checkout**. Nobody
else creates them, and they are never committed, attached to an issue or written into a log. In
CI they exist only as secrets of the protected `android-signing` environment of the store
delivery workflow ([ci-delivery.md](../ci-delivery.md), issue #90).

**App signing key:** the existing procedure in the
[signing guide §1–§2](../android-release-signing.md#1-create-the-key-once-), including the two
offline backups and the recovery drill. One key for all three stores.

**Upload key** (separate keystore, separate password):

```bash
keytool -genkeypair -v -storetype PKCS12 \
  -keystore talentforge-upload.p12 -alias talentforge-upload \
  -keyalg RSA -keysize 4096 -validity 36500 \
  -dname "CN=<owner name>, O=TalentForge, C=<country code>"
keytool -list -v -keystore talentforge-upload.p12 -alias talentforge-upload | grep 'SHA256:'
keytool -export -rfc -keystore talentforge-upload.p12 -alias talentforge-upload \
  -file talentforge-upload-certificate.pem
```

- keytool prompts for the password; do not type it on the command line. Generate it in the
  password manager, different from the app signing key's.
- The DN is public (it is in every uploaded bundle). No e-mail or street address.
- Back it up like the app signing key (two encrypted offline copies). Losing it is recoverable (§7),
  but costs a support request and a delay.
- Record both fingerprints, which are public, in the release record:
  `APP_SIGNING_SHA256` and `UPLOAD_SHA256`. They **must differ**.

## 5. Handing the app signing key to Google Play 🔑

### 5.1 What leaves the owner's machine

Only an **encrypted** export of the app signing key, made by Google's PEPK tool with the
encryption public key that Play Console shows for this app, plus the upload certificate
(`.pem`, public). The keystore file and its password never leave the machine.

### 5.2 Steps (owner, in Play Console; nothing here was executed)

1. Create the app (package name `io.github.alexeykravtsov95.talentforge` is set by the first
   bundle and can never change). See [closed-testing §2](closed-testing.md#2-create-the-app-in-play-console-).
2. **Before uploading any bundle:** open *Protected with Play* → *Play Store distribution* → *Go
   to Play app signing* → *Change the app signing key*.
3. Choose the option to provide a copy of your own key from a Java keystore (Google calls it
   "Export and upload a key from Java keystore"). Download the PEPK tool and the encryption public
   key **from that page**. Run the command **exactly as the page shows it**. Its shape is:

   ```bash
   java -jar pepk.jar --keystore=talentforge-release.p12 --alias=talentforge \
     --output=talentforge-app-signing-key.zip --include-cert --rsa-aes-encryption \
     --encryption-key-path=encryption_public_key.pem
   ```

   Run it on the release machine, in a directory outside every checkout. Delete the `.zip` after
   the upload succeeds.
4. Before confirming, read the screen for U-1…U-3 (§3). If Play says it will **still** generate a
   classical key of its own, or if the owner's key cannot be used for the classical signature,
   **stop**: that breaks the three-store scheme. Record what the screen says and take the decision
   back to the owner (P-1).
5. Upload `talentforge-app-signing-key.zip` and register the upload certificate
   `talentforge-upload-certificate.pem`.
6. Verify on *App signing*:
   - *App signing key certificate* SHA-256 = `APP_SIGNING_SHA256`;
   - *Upload key certificate* SHA-256 = `UPLOAD_SHA256`.

   Screenshot the page for the release record. This is the only proof that Play will sign with the
   owner's key.
7. Only now build and upload the first bundle (§6). After the first closed-test install, check the
   delivered certificate on a device
   ([multi-store §8](../appgallery/multi-store-signing.md#8-certificate-sha-256-verification), step 3):
   it must be `APP_SIGNING_SHA256`, the same as an install from RuStore or AppGallery.

## 6. Building the Google Play bundle

### 6.1 Settings

The upload key is configured exactly like the release key, under its own names, in the
environment or `~/.gradle/gradle.properties` (never in the repository):

| Environment variable | Gradle property | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `TALENTFORGE_PLAY_UPLOAD_STORE_FILE` | `talentforge.play.upload.store_file` | absolute path of `talentforge-upload.p12`, outside the checkout |
| `TALENTFORGE_PLAY_UPLOAD_STORE_PASSWORD` | `talentforge.play.upload.store_password` | upload keystore password |
| `TALENTFORGE_PLAY_UPLOAD_KEY_ALIAS` | `talentforge.play.upload.key_alias` | `talentforge-upload` |
| `TALENTFORGE_PLAY_UPLOAD_KEY_PASSWORD` | `talentforge.play.upload.key_password` | key password (= store password for PKCS12) |
| `TALENTFORGE_PLAY_UPLOAD_CERT_SHA256` | `talentforge.play.upload.cert_sha256` | `UPLOAD_SHA256` (public) |
| `TALENTFORGE_RELEASE_CERT_SHA256` | `talentforge.release.cert_sha256` | `APP_SIGNING_SHA256` (public), which the bundle must **not** carry |

The app signing **keystore** is not needed to build the bundle; only its public fingerprint is.
A `talentforge.release.*` or `talentforge.play.*` key in the repository's `gradle.properties` fails
the build.

### 6.2 Tasks

| Task | Settings | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `assemblePublishableRelease` (unchanged) | release key + `APP_SIGNING_SHA256` | `build/outputs/publishable/TalentForge-<v>-<code>.apk` for RuStore and AppGallery. With `TALENTFORGE_PLAY_UPLOAD_CERT_SHA256` set, it also refuses an APK signed with the upload key |
| **`bundlePublishablePlay`** | upload key + `UPLOAD_SHA256` + `APP_SIGNING_SHA256` | `build/outputs/publishable-google-play/TalentForge-<v>-<code>.aab` and its `.report.json`: **the only file to upload to Google Play** |
| `bundlePlay` | none or upload key | technical bundle (`outputs/bundle/play/`). Never uploaded: unverified, unsigned when no settings are set |
| `bundleRelease` | release key | a bundle signed with the **app signing key**. **Never uploaded to Play**: the validator rejects it (self-test scenario `release-aab`) |
| `assemblePlay` / `packagePlay` | any | **fails** (`refusePlayApk`): the `play` build type packages no APK, so an upload-key APK can never reach RuStore or AppGallery |

How the bundle is built: a `play` build type that copies `release` (`initWith`) and differs only
in its signing configuration (the upload key, or none). It uses the same sources, OTA settings
and NET-1 manifest overlay, and the same checks (`checkReleaseOtaSettings`,
`checkPlayInternetPermission`). AGP signs per build type, so the two keys cannot be mixed inside
one variant. Signing the `release` bundle and re-signing it outside Gradle was rejected: it would
first produce a bundle signed with the app signing key.

```bash
cd mobile
./gradlew :composeApp:bundlePublishablePlay
```

### 6.3 What the publishable bundle must pass (`scripts/verify_play_bundle.py`)

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| `signature-verifies`, `all-entries-signed`, `single-signer` | `jarsigner -verify` succeeds; every entry is in the signed JAR manifest; exactly one signer and one certificate |
| `not-debug-certificate-dn`, `not-local-debug-certificate` | never the Android debug key |
| `upload-certificate-sha256-matches` | the certificate equals `UPLOAD_SHA256` |
| `certificate-differs-from-other-role` | the certificate is **not** `APP_SIGNING_SHA256` |
| `rsa-key-size` | RSA, at least 2048 bits |
| `not-selftest-certificate` | not a temporary self-test key (§8) |
| `application-id`, `version-code`, `version-name` | `io.github.alexeykravtsov95.talentforge`; the build's version code and name |
| `target-sdk` | at least 36 (Google Play, since 2026-08-31) |
| `not-debuggable`, `not-test-only` | release manifest only |
| `permissions-allowed` | only the AndroidX `…DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION`; `INTERNET` only while production OTA is enabled (NET-1) |
| `abi-arm64-v8a`, `abi-64-bit-coverage` | 64-bit code for every packaged 32-bit ABI |
| `native-16kb-page-size`, `bundle-native-page-alignment` | every 64-bit `.so` has ELF `LOAD` alignment ≥ 16 KB; BundleConfig stores native libraries uncompressed at `PAGE_ALIGNMENT_16K` |

The bundle manifest and `BundleConfig.pb` are protocol buffers, so `aapt2 dump badging` cannot
read a bundle. The validator decodes them itself; its decoding is pinned against the real
`bundlePlay` manifest, which `bundletool dump manifest` also read
(`tests/fixtures/play_bundle`). No bundletool installation is needed.

**Fail-closed staging.** Gradle clears `outputs/publishable-google-play/` as soon as it plans a
build that includes `bundlePublishablePlay`, before any task runs. The task deletes the directory
again on any failure. A failed attempt therefore never leaves an earlier bundle there (self-test
scenario `stale`).

Before each upload, re-check the exact file standalone and record its SHA-256:

```bash
python3 scripts/verify_play_bundle.py --aab TalentForge-<v>-<code>.aab \
  --expected-upload-cert-sha256 <UPLOAD_SHA256> --app-signing-cert-sha256 <APP_SIGNING_SHA256> \
  --report /tmp/play-report.json
```

### 6.4 Versions across the three stores

The `versionCode` sequence is shared: one number per built artifact, strictly increasing, never
reused. The publishable APK and the Play bundle of one release are built from **one commit**
with **one `versionCode`/`versionName`**, and the self-test checks that the two reports agree
(scenario `metadata`). Closed-test builds on Play consume numbers too: the production bundle needs
a higher `versionCode` than every bundle uploaded to any Play track. First release (M5.RELEASE.VERSION):
test `1` / `1.0.0`, public `2` / `1.0.0`; the procedure and the ledger are in
[android-versioning.md](../android-versioning.md).

## 7. Losing or leaking a key

| Event | Effect | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Upload key lost or leaked | No effect on installs. New bundles cannot be uploaded until reset | Play Console → *Protected with Play* → *Play Store protection* → *Manage Play app signing* → *Request upload key reset*: create a new upload key (§4), upload its `keytool -export -rfc` PEM, give the reason; update `TALENTFORGE_PLAY_UPLOAD_*` and `UPLOAD_SHA256` |
| App signing key lost | Play keeps signing (it holds a copy). RuStore and AppGallery updates end ([multi-store §10](../appgallery/multi-store-signing.md#10-consequences-of-losing-or-leaking-the-signing-key)) | the backups and recovery drill are the only protection |
| App signing key leaked | anyone can sign an APK that Android accepts as a TalentForge update outside the stores | stop releasing; contact all three stores' support; decide on a key change with the consequences in multi-store §10 |

## 8. Self-test without production keys

`scripts/play_signing_selftest.sh` creates **two temporary RSA 4096 keys**, an "app signing test
key" and an "upload test key". Both carry the subject marker `TalentForge signing self-test`, and
each has its own random password that exists only in the script's environment. The script then
drives the real Gradle tasks and both validators. Results: [readiness §2](readiness.md#2-engineering-passfail),
evidence: [evidence/play-signing-selftest/](evidence/play-signing-selftest/).

The scenarios show that:
- the APK carries the app signing key and the AAB the upload key;
- the two certificates differ;
- the release metadata of the two artifacts is equal;
- a wrong fingerprint, the same fingerprint for both roles, the release key as upload key, and an
  AAB or APK signed with the other role's key all fail;
- missing, partial and incomplete settings fail;
- unsigned, `bundleRelease` and debug bundles are rejected;
- a `play` APK is refused;
- no stale bundle survives a failed attempt;
- the logs and `build/` hold no secret;
- the keys and all test-signed outputs are deleted at the end.

**A test artifact can never pass as a release.** The validators accept a self-test certificate
only with `--selftest`, which Gradle passes only while `TALENTFORGE_SIGNING_SELFTEST=true`. In that
mode:
- the report's `result` is `SELFTEST-PASS`, never `PASS`;
- `purpose` is `signing-selftest`;
- the staged files are named `*-SELFTEST-NOT-FOR-UPLOAD.apk/.aab`.

Without the switch, both publishable tasks refuse the test keys and stage nothing (scenario
`not-selftest`). With the switch, a real key is refused (`selftest-certificate`). The test
certificates are valid for 2 days and are deleted with their keys.

`mobile-ci` runs both self-tests with their temporary keys only. Production signing secrets are
used by nothing but the owner-started store delivery workflow ([ci-delivery.md](../ci-delivery.md)).

## 9. Never

- Let Play generate the app signing key, or accept a key upgrade, without an explicit owner
  decision (§3).
- Upload `composeApp-release.aab`, `composeApp-play.aab`, anything named `*-SELFTEST-NOT-FOR-UPLOAD*`,
  or anything not produced by `bundlePublishablePlay` from the release commit.
- Upload the APK to Google Play (new apps require bundles), or give RuStore or AppGallery anything
  signed with the upload key.
- Commit, paste or log a keystore, a password or a PEPK `.zip`; put signing secrets into CI
  outside the protected environments of [ci-delivery.md](../ci-delivery.md).
